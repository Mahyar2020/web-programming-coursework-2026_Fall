# Editorial and Teaching Style

## Voice and learning progression

Write as an experienced professor speaking directly and respectfully to an
intelligent beginner. Begin with a concrete situation or observable result,
introduce one necessary term at a time, explain its parts and purpose, and then
connect it to the complete system.

Prefer clear narrative prose over note fragments. Explain why distinctions matter
and name the limits of simplified teaching models. Avoid filler such as
"obviously," "simply," and "just."

## Terminology and notation

- Expand an abbreviation at its first meaningful use in every chapter.
- Define unfamiliar terms before depending on them.
- Format literal commands, filenames, addresses, URLs, fields, and code as code.
- Explain punctuation when it has technical meaning.
- Avoid punctuation layouts that a novice could mistake for part of a literal
  value.

## Examples and activities

Use a coherent continuing example when the subject permits it. Explain every
meaningful part of a learner's first example. Test executable material and show
the expected evidence. Distinguish source, configuration, terminal output,
program output, and generated illustration.

Laboratories should include prerequisites, exact starting location, numbered
actions, expected evidence, checkpoints, troubleshooting, cleanup, and a complete
worked solution. Exercises and quizzes must align with learning outcomes. Answers
must explain reasoning and common misconceptions rather than merely state results.

## Figures and tables

- Use chapter-local decimal numbering, beginning at 1 in every chapter.
- Number figures and tables independently and use numeric identifiers only.
- Give every instructional figure and table a descriptive caption.
- Refer to each visual by number in the surrounding prose.
- Explain whether components work together, sequentially, independently, or as
  alternatives.
- Label important objects, actions, messages, and paths.
- Keep labels, connectors, arrowheads, and decorative objects inside the boundary.
- Use accessible contrast and do not encode meaning by color alone.
- Use authentic screenshots for visible executable output.
- Do not print detached labels such as "Text alternative:" in the book.

## Sources

Use official or primary sources for current behavior. Use university materials
and established books to compare pedagogy and scope. Paraphrase in the author's
own voice, respect licenses and quotation limits, and annotate references so the
reader knows why each one is useful.
